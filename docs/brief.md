# 掛襟シャツ — Complete Project Brief

A modified jinbei top: keeps the thermal engineering of a jinbei, drops the features that read as festival wear. Built as a work-wearable summer shirt for an office in Kansai.

This document is a handoff. It contains the design rationale, every measurement, the cutting layout, the full build sequence, and the reasoning behind each decision — so someone picking this up cold can continue it.

---

# PART 1 — What this garment is

## The problem

Jinbei are the coolest summer garment available, but they read as festival wear or pajamas and can't be worn to an office. The goal was to keep the cooling mechanisms while removing the cultural signals.

## The key insight

A jinbei's cooling features and its "festival" signals are largely **the same features**. Listed by mechanism:

**Fabric**
1. しじら織り — puckered weave, so only the ridges touch skin
2. Low thread count, open plain weave — air permeability
3. Loosely twisted yarn — traps air, absorbs faster
4. Cotton or 綿麻 — absorbs sweat and releases it, so evaporation happens off the skin
5. 単衣 — completely unlined

**Cut**
6. Rectangular panels, no darts — cloth hangs straight, standing air layer between fabric and torso
7. Generous ease, 10–20 cm more than a shirt — volume enables convection
8. 筒袖 straight sleeve, wide and open at the cuff — acts as a bellows

**Openings — where jinbei beats 作務衣**
9. 身八つ口 — vertical slit in the body panel under the arm
10. Short 袖付け — sleeve attached only across part of the armhole, leaving a permanent gap
11. かがり — decorative thread lacing at the sleeve join, functionally mesh
12. Side-seam gaps at the 紐
13. Deep V neckline — the exhaust port
14. Open, ungathered hem — air intake

**Closure**
15. 紐 ties — no continuous closed placket
16. Drawstring waist on the trousers

Ranked by actual thermal contribution: **underarm venting (9, 10), volume (6, 7), and fabric crinkle (1)** do most of the work.

**The collision:** items 9, 11, 15 and the striped version of 1 are simultaneously the best cooling features and the strongest festival tells.

## The design solution

| Jinbei feature | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 前合わせ diagonal overlap | **Removed** | Thermally near-neutral (double layer over the belly is a small negative). The V and the side gap were doing the cooling, not the overlap |
| 衽 panel | **Deleted** | This is the piece that creates the overlap |
| Front closure | **Center-front buttons** | ~2 cm placket overlap instead of 18 cm panel overlap |
| V neckline | **Kept** | Real cooling value — it's the exhaust vent. Preserved via a short diagonal at the top of the front edge |
| 身八つ口 | **Closed** | Strong festival tell |
| Open 袖付け | **Kept, 6 cm** | The single most important cooling feature. Replaces the 身八つ口 |
| かがり lacing | **Kept as 千鳥かがり** on the underarm vent | Functional venting; tonal thread keeps the 和 signal moderate |
| Side vents | **18 cm at hem** | Replaces the 紐 gap as low-level air intake |
| 衿 | **掛襟 band, 4 cm** | 半纏 construction — meets edge to edge at CF, no wrap. Narrower than a real 半纏's 8–10 cm to keep the 和 signal at ~30% |
| 筒袖 | **Kept, straightened** | Flare removed (a tell), tube kept |
| Underarm gusset | **Added** | Not traditional. Structural — gives the vent's bar-tack corner something sound to sit against |
| Striped しじら | **Solid black** | Wide stripe is the single biggest festival signal |

**Target read:** modern heritage workwear. Nearest real equivalents are 45R, Blue Blue Japan, Universal Works band-collar shirts, or a European grandad shirt. Deliberately *not* readable as jinbei, 作務衣, ryokan staff, or 唐装/中山装.

**Things to avoid that would break the read:** matched top-and-bottom set in the same 和 cut (reads as uniform), 前掛け, 足袋 with sandals, indigo + 刺子 + asymmetric hem in combination, high closed collar with sheen (reads 中山装), 地下足袋.

# PART 2 — Materials and equipment

Linen-dominant was chosen deliberately: linen's moisture absorption and fast release is the dominant cooling mechanism for someone who sweats heavily. No crinkle (not しじら), so roughly 85% as cool as しじら would be — but the crisp hand partly compensates, and the extra body helps the collar band hold its shape.

**Note for a future version:** solid 綿麻しじら is rare online because the しじら technique grew up inside striped cloth, all its traditional end uses are striped, linen dyes unevenly across large solid panels, and when mills do make solid crinkled 綿麻 they sell it as ワッシャー加工 / リップル / 楊柳 rather than しじら. ## The front panel — the critical piece

The centre front edge has **two sections**, exactly as on a jinbei but with the transition raised:

- **Vertical**, straight, from the hem up to the 剣先 at 18 cm below the shoulder line
- **Diagonal**, from the 剣先 out to the neck point 8 cm along the shoulder

This is the whole modification. A real jinbei's transition sits near the hem, so the diagonal covers the whole chest. Raising it to 18 cm gives a V opening at the top and a straight buttonable edge below.

**The 剣先 must be rounded, not sharp.** Blend the vertical into the diagonal over about 8 cm of gentle arc — roughly the curve of a coffee mug. Reason: the 掛襟 is a flat straight strip, and a flat strip cannot turn a sharp corner without bunching. A gentle curve it can follow, with steam.

The V still reads as a V. The band's own 4 cm width blunts the junction anyway, so a sharp pattern corner would not produce a sharp finished point.

**No front neck scoop needed.** The diagonal creates the opening. Only the back gets a scoop.

**V depth is adjustable** via the 剣先: 12 cm = shallow and buttoned-up, 18 cm = balanced, 25 cm = deep and more jinbei-like.

## 掛襟 length calculation

| Section | Length |
|---|---|
| CF vertical, hem to 剣先 (70 − 18) | 52 cm |
| Diagonal, √(18² + 8²) | 19.7 cm |
| One front edge | 71.7 cm |
| Both fronts | 143.4 cm |
| Back neck arc | 21.5 cm |
| **Total seam length** | **165 cm** |
| **Cut length** | **170 cm** (5 cm for trimming ends) |

**Formula if dimensions change:** band = 2 × (body length − 剣先 depth + diagonal) + back neck arc + 5.

Cut as **one piece** running lengthwise on the bolt. No centre-back join.

## Cutting layout — 140 cm bolt

All pieces fit within **172 cm** of length. Purchased 2.5 m, so ~78 cm remains for practice scraps and error recovery.

Across the 140 cm width, first section: えり band (15) + 後ろ身頃 (49) + 前身頃 (28) + 前身頃 (28) = 120 cm. Second section below: そで (47) + そで (47) + まち = 94 cm.

**Grain runs along the bolt length on every piece, no exceptions.** On 55% linen with a visible weave, a crosswise piece looks noticeably different.

**Cut the back on the fold** (fold its 49 cm section in half) so the neck scoop is symmetrical. **Cut the two fronts folded together** so they mirror perfectly.

**Mark before unpinning:** armhole stop 24 cm down from the shoulder on all four side edges, and vent start 18 cm up from the hem. Snip 3 mm into the seam allowance at each — 8 notches. These four bar-tack points are the difference between a garment that vents and one with holes in it.

# PART 4 — Build sequence

## STEP 1 — 水通し (day the fabric arrives)

Pre-shrinking, before cutting. Linen shrinks 3–5% and unevenly; a garment cut dry will twist permanently after its first wash. Also straightens the grain so straight cuts come out straight.

1. Fill the bath with lukewarm water. Submerge the whole uncut fabric, push it fully under. **Leave 1 hour.**
2. Lift out — **do not wring.** Twisting crushes hard creases into the weave.
3. Machine 脱水 **30–60 seconds only.** Longer packs the fabric against the drum and sets fold lines. Load it loosely, spread out. (Alternative: roll in a dry towel and press.)
4. Hang damp **over a pole** so no crease line forms. Not pinned by one edge — 1.7 m of wet linen is heavy and will stretch at the pin points.
5. Pull it square: tug the selvedges crosswise, then lengthwise. If one corner hangs low, pull the opposite corners diagonally. Smooth by hand from the centre out. Keep out of direct sun.
6. Take down while still **damp**, not dry. Press dry along the grain (parallel to the selvedge) with the 当て布.

## STEP 2 — Machine setup

1. Run the machine over a fabric scrap with **no thread** to clear factory oil.
2. Fit the **#11 needle** — flat side of the shank facing away, pushed fully up.
3. Wind a bobbin, fit the bobbin case, thread the top.
4. Test seam on two layers of scrap. If bobbin thread shows on top, lower the 上糸調子 from the default 5; if top thread shows underneath, raise it.

## STEP 3 — Verify measurements

Measure a shirt that fits well, flat, and compare against Part 3. Adjust before cutting.

## STEP 4 — Mark and cut

1. Single layer, right side up, on the mat.
2. Make a small paper template for the front top corner only — the diagonal plus the rounded 剣先 plus the shoulder slope. Everything else is marked directly with the 方眼定規.
3. Mark all pieces with seam allowances included.
4. Mark and notch the 8 armhole-stop and vent-start points.
5. Cut the back neck scoop after the fronts are done, to avoid cutting a deeper curve into the wrong piece.
6. Rotary cutter against the ruler for straight lines; scissors for curves.

## STEP 5 — Test sandwich (before touching real pieces)

Build a scrap stack identical to the real 掛襟 — folded fabric plus interfacing, same total thickness.

1. Sew a 30 cm test band onto a scrap edge. Observe where it puckers.
2. Fit the ボタンホール押え. Clamp an actual button between つめA and つめB.
3. Lower the **ボタンホールレバー fully** — the machine will not run if it is partway down.
4. Sew **six buttonholes.** Cut each open with the seam ripper, placing a pin across each end as a stop.
5. Check against a real button: it should pass with slight resistance.

**Do not proceed until buttonholes come out clean and consistent.** This is the highest-risk operation on the garment and the only one done at the very end on a finished piece.

## STEP 6 — 刺し子 back stitching (optional)

Single row of running stitch across the back panel, 8 cm below the neckline, full width. 5 mm stitch length, 刺し子糸 one shade off the fabric. Do it while the panel is flat.

Purely decorative — it sits where a Western shirt's yoke seam would be, giving the plain back a horizontal line that reads as heritage workwear.

## STEP 7 — Gussets into sleeves

Fold each 8 × 8 cm gusset on the diagonal and set it into the underarm junction where the sleeve underseam will meet the side seam. Press seams open.

Structural, not decorative — the vent's bar-tack corner is a stress point and would tear without it.

## STEP 8 — Sleeves to body

Right sides together, sleeve's straight edge to the body's straight armhole edge. Sew from the shoulder point downward and **stop 6 cm short of the bottom.** Backstitch. Four armhole edges total. Press open.

The armhole is a straight vertical edge, not a curve — there is no sleeve cap and no easing.

## STEP 9 — Finish the underarm vents

Turn both raw edges of each 6 cm opening under 1 cm twice and topstitch. **Bar-tack horizontally** where the seam stitching stopped — machine zigzag at 0 stitch length, or by hand.

This bar-tack is what stops the vent tearing. Do not skip it.

## STEP 10 — Side seams

Sew from the gusset down to **18 cm above the hem.** Backstitch. Press open.

## STEP 11 — Hem vents

Turn the vent edges under 1 cm twice, topstitch, bar-tack horizontally at the top of each opening.

## STEP 12 — Hems

- **Sleeve cuffs:** fold 2 cm, fold 2 cm again, topstitch once. Plain hem — no band, no button, no elastic. A straight open tube is what makes the bellows effect work.
- **Sleeve hem slit:** 5 cm at the outer-seam end of each cuff. Turn edges, stitch, bar-tack the top. Lets the tube open when the arm swings.
- **Body hem:** fold 1.5 cm twice, topstitch once. Straight and level all round — no shirt-tail curve.

## STEP 13 — 掛襟 (the hard part)

1. Fuse interfacing along the strip — at minimum the buttonhole section, whole length if the band feels floppy. Cut interfacing with the grain running the same way as the strip.
2. Press the strip: fold in half lengthwise, then fold each raw edge in. Spray with water, press, and **let it cool in position before moving it.** Cooling is what sets the fold.
3. **Pre-shape the band before pinning.** At the back neck (concave) shrink the inner edge with steam; at the rounded 剣先 (convex) stretch the outer edge. Press into the curve, let cool. 55% linen shapes readily. A straight strip on a shallow curve is standard tailoring — this is why the curves were kept shallow.
4. Pin or clip from one hem, up the front, around the back neck, down the other front.
5. **Baste with しつけ糸** — 1 cm running stitches, 2 mm closer to the raw edge than the seam line. Remove all pins.
6. Machine-sew slowly. Every 20 cm, check the strip end still aligns with the garment edge.
7. Make the **right band 2 cm wider** where it crosses centre front — that is the overlap the buttons sit on. Men's convention: buttonholes left, buttons right.
8. Snip and pull the basting out.
9. Press with the 当て布.

**Why basting matters here specifically:** the feed dogs grip only the bottom layer, so it travels further than the top. Over a 72 cm band that accumulates to 5–15 mm (縫いズレ), and the band ends up not matching the edge it is sewn to.

## STEP 14 — Buttonholes and buttons

1. Mark **all four positions before sewing any.** First at 2–3 cm below the 剣先, then every 9 cm. Nothing above the first button — that gap is the V.
2. Sew all four with the automatic function, same button in the clamp throughout, so all four are identical.
3. Cut each open with a pin across each end as a stop. Overshooting by 2 mm cuts the bar tack and the buttonhole unravels — unrepairable.
4. Sew buttons onto the right band using the ボタン付け押え and ダーニングプレート, low speed.

**Buttonhole length** = button diameter + thickness + 2 mm. The automatic function derives this from the clamped button.

**Fallback if buttonholes fail:** snaps behind the band with buttons sewn on top as decoration. Legitimate construction, invisible in wear, used in ready-to-wear.

## STEP 15 — 千鳥かがり

1. Mark stitch positions along both edges of each underarm vent with チャコペン and ruler. **Even spacing is what makes it look right** — uneven spacing is what makes かがり look amateur.
2. Thread a long hand needle with 50–60 cm of レース糸 #20. (Short lengths instead of using thread wax.)
3. Anchor with a knot pulled into the seam allowance.
4. Work crossed stitches back and forth across the gap, offsetting the needle insertion points to produce the crossing.

**Thread colour is the 和 dial:** white is the classic jinbei look and the loudest 祭 signal; tonal (one or two shades off the fabric) gives the texture without it. Ecru sits in between.

## STEP 16 — Final press

Press every seam and fold with the 当て布, from the wrong side where possible. Hang and let cool completely before wearing.

# PART 5 — Rules that apply throughout

- **Press every seam as you sew it.** Well-pressed mediocre stitching beats sloppy-pressed perfect stitching. This is a discipline, not a skill.
- **Spray, press, let cool.** Moving fabric while warm and damp undoes the fold entirely.
- **当て布 on every press to the outside.** Direct iron on black linen causes permanent shine (アタリ). Wrong-side pressing of seam allowances does not need it.
- **Test on scrap first** whenever attempting something new.
- **Slow down for the band and the buttonholes.** Everything else is forgiving.
- **Buy a second rotary blade before starting.** Linen dulls the first faster than expected, and a dragging blade chews a panel that cannot be recut.

## Expected first-attempt flaws

Likely: wobbly topstitching, some puckering where the band crosses the shoulders, one or two buttonholes slightly off, minor asymmetry. All normal.

Unlikely, by design: fit failure (20 cm of ease absorbs error), unfixable cutting error (all rectangles, spare fabric), fabric ruined (dark linen hides much, 水通し prevents twisting).

The real risk is impatience — skipping the press after each seam, or cutting the real fabric before the scrap tests. Both are decisions, not abilities.

## Estimated time

12–18 hours for a first attempt, across two or three weekends. 5–6 hours for someone experienced.

# PART 7 — Open questions for a future version

- **Fabric:** find solid 綿麻しじら, 楊柳, or 阿波しじら 反物 for the crinkle. Requires an in-person visit to 大塚屋 京橋 or similar.
- **反物 route:** a genuine 和裁 draft at 36–40 cm bolt width, with a centre-back seam and 繰り越し (shifting the shoulder fold 2–3 cm rearward to drop the neckline back instead of cutting a curve). Needs ~4.5 m and a different layout entirely.
- **Sharp 剣先:** mitre or seam the band at the corner instead of rounding, once the band's behaviour is understood.
- **内紐:** hidden ties inside the left front and right side seam, if the fronts shift when sitting.
- **Trousers:** もんぺ or wide straight-leg in the same cloth. Harder than the shirt — the crotch curve is the only genuinely curved seam, and the rise has no ease budget to absorb error. But no buttonholes and no collar.
- **Documented 型紙:** the reasoning in Part 1 — which jinbei features carry thermal load versus cultural signal — does not appear to exist in published form. That, rather than the garment, may be the thing worth publishing.
